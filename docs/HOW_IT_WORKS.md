# How it works

These are the engineering notes behind the stitcher: what the `.OSV` file contains, how each constant was measured, and which approaches failed and why. They are detailed on purpose. Almost every line records a measurement, and several record a mistake that looked right until it was measured.

All of it was established on **one first-generation DJI Osmo 360 body**. Nothing here has been checked against the Osmo 360 II.

## The file format

An `.OSV` is a normal MP4 container holding two 3840x3840 HEVC 10-bit circular fisheye tracks (one per lens), AAC audio, DJI `djmd` protobuf metadata carrying per-lens factory calibration plus a ~1 kHz IMU stream, two `dbgi` sensor-debug tracks (~4.2 Mbps, discardable), and a single stitched equirectangular cover JPEG. There is no public schema for the metadata; the field paths below were found by decoding it.

## Lens model and calibration

- Per-lens calibration lives at `djmd` protobuf path `top.2.6.*` — `fx, fy` (field 1/2), `cx, cy` (3/4), four distortion coefficients (5–8), frame size (10/11), yaw/pitch (12/13), inter-lens extrinsic quaternion (21, `[w,x,y,z]`), and a 14-float circle in fields 22/23. The block repeats once per capture mode, so the script keeps the first pair with opposing optical axes.

- **The lens is a Mei unified omnidirectional model** (`radial_fn()`), recovered from DJI Studio's own calibration bundle: `rho = sin(θ)/(cos(θ)+ξ)`, radial-distorted by `(1 + k1·rho² + k2·rho⁴)`, then a per-body pixel scale. For the Osmo 360 `MEI_XI = 0.713`, `MEI_K1 = -0.183`, `MEI_K2 = 0.0184` (lens-design constants, both lenses agree to <1%; baked in). Only the pixel scale is per body — `mei_scale()` fits it to that body's own djmd factory polynomial over 2–70°. The model is monotonic and well-defined all the way to the rim.

- **This replaced the earlier OpenCV-fisheye θ-polynomial reading**, which was only trustworthy to ~80°: past it the polynomial's slope collapsed and it *turned over* near 88–92° (radius decreasing with angle — impossible for a fisheye), so the whole 88–96° overlap band (exactly where the lenses blend) had to be faked with a hand-fit straight line (the old `DEFAULT_EDGE_SLOPE = 1.28×fx`, once "the single most important constant in the file"). The Mei model needs no extrapolation. Validated cross-independently on the test camera: it matches the OSV's own per-body polynomial to **~2.5 px RMS over 2–70°**, stays monotonic to 96°, lands within **0.4%** of the measured image circle at θ=93° (a ~186° fisheye), and **cuts cross-lens seam disagreement 26.9%** (0.2165→0.1583) vs the old model — landing at DJI's own reference seam metric (~0.15).

- **The rim still needed a per-body scale: `DEFAULT_RADIUS_MULT = 0.992`.** The Mei scale is fitted to the factory polynomial over 2–70°, but stitching only cares where θ≈90° lands, and on the test camera it landed ~0.8% too far out. Symptom: far scenery (hills at 60/120/200 s) sat **~1.7° off along both seams with opposite signs** (+1.9° seam 0, −1.6° seam 1) — opposite signs across the two seams is the signature of a radial-scale error (a relative lens yaw gives the same sign on both). The seam optical flow had been silently absorbing this static offset, and wherever it could not (dark, low-texture ground rows) the seam ghosted — that was the detached-heel ghost on the person at the seam. A brute-force far-scenery shift search crosses zero at 0.9914/0.9927; at 0.992 the residual is ≤0.5° in every latitude band from +55° to −50° on both seams over six frames, so the flow now carries only real parallax.

- **The fields 22/23 circle is not a reliable anchor.** Only reported by `inspect`, not used by the stitch. The Mei model instead lands on the lut's measured image-circle radius to within 0.4% as a *consequence*, not by anchoring.

- **`--calibrate` measures that rim scale** (`calibrate_radius_mult`): reads three LRF frames, and for each candidate scale in `RADIUS_MULT_RANGE = (0.975, 1.015)` runs the far-scenery shift search on the +55…−50° band of both seams, keeping the scale where the two offsets vanish together; a hit on either bound is treated as a failed measurement and the default kept. Use it for another camera body. The earlier `--calibrate` searched the Mei ξ against a whole-band texture-disagreement metric and never detected the 1.7° offset — that metric is dominated by content, not geometry. `--edge-slope` is a retained no-op. Note the measurement wants *far* content in the band: a person standing in the seam biases a frame by ~0.5°, which is why several frames are combined.

- **Handedness**: `WORLD_ROT` is a reflection (det = −1), not a rotation. Equirect longitude runs the opposite way round the sphere from the extrinsics' handedness; without the x flip the whole panorama comes out **mirrored**. This is invisible on symmetric scenery and obvious the moment any text is in shot — verify with a shot containing legible text, not a generic interior.

- **Yaw**: `default_yaw()` centres the front lens (the one with yaw ≈ 0). Without it the rear lens lands dead centre and the output sits ~180° from how DJI Studio frames the same clip.

- **All samples validated so far come from one camera body** (identical `fx`/`cx`/distortion across three clips). The Mei `MEI_XI/K1/K2` are lens-design constants (both lenses on this body agree to <1%), so per-body generalisation should hold, but a second body is the test that matters; if its optimum `ξ` differs, `--calibrate` is the escape hatch.

## Seams: parallax compensation

- **Seam flow (parallax compensation) is implemented and on by default** (`--seam flow`; `static` restores the plain cross-fade). The lenses do not share an entrance pupil, so near content lands at different positions in the two hemispheres and a static 10° cross-fade double-exposes it into the smeared "visible stitch" band. Seam flow measures a **dense 2-D optical flow** (OpenCV DIS on CLAHE-equalised views — Farnebäck was tried first and returned an exact zero on the dark, low-contrast ground rows below −60°, so the nadir cut got no shift there and a foot on the cut line was duplicated as a detached heel; DIS recovers a consistent ground parallax of ~1.3–1.7° physical there, growing toward the nadir with opposite sign on the two seams, and is ~3× faster) between the two lens views across the whole seam band per frame, and warps the two lenses toward each other inside the band before blending: lens 0 sampled at `+d·m`, lens 1 at `−d·(1−m)` where `m` is the blend weight, so the difference is exactly `d` wherever both are visible and the band edges stay continuous. This is the mechanism behind DJI Studio's seam optical flow.

- **The waving was the 1-D field shearing, plus fixed-window temporal smoothing.** The first design used a **1-D per-latitude offset** — each latitude shifted independently, which shears a vertical edge, and as the offset changed frame to frame the shear rippled into the visible seam *waving*. A gaussian/EMA temporal smooth over `--seam-smooth` did not remove it. The fix (the approach DJI Studio's own stitcher takes): a **coherent 2-D field over the whole band** (edges stay straight, only translate) plus a **random-walk Kalman filter + RTS smoother** (`kalman_flow_smooth`) whose measurement noise scales inversely with per-sample confidence — far/low-texture pixels settle to a steady value (no waving), confident close parallax is followed without lag. `--seam-smooth` now scales the Kalman process noise. Validated on the walking clip: the seam band's frame-to-frame temporal std matches a static seam to within +0.01–0.12 (the warp adds no temporal instability), while the parallax is still corrected.

- **The dense 2-D field covers the whole seam, asymmetrically**: from the zenith taper (`SEAM_FLOW_TAPER`, fades to zero across +45…+55° where the overlap shrinks and sky needs no warp) all the way **down to −82°** (`SEAM_FLOW_LAT_MAX`, with a short fade in the last degrees). The nadir side must not be tapered: close parallax lives at −40…−75° (the wearer's body, the ground, a person next to the camera). A revision that applied the taper to |lat| on both sides silently stopped correcting anything below −45° and brought the ghosting back on a person straddling the seam (torso and legs doubled, a translucent band on the jacket edge) — restoring the nadir rows took the seam residual at those latitudes from ~17 to ~8 and the person renders solid again. The flow is also zeroed within `SEAM_FLOW_EDGE_ERODE` samples of a lens's validity edge, where the flow estimator otherwise fills its window with a clamp-hitting bogus flow from the black region outside the other lens. It is measured on the LRF decode, clamped per latitude to 2.5 **physical** degrees (`SEAM_FLOW_MAX_DEG`, ×1/cos(lat) in longitude, capped at 6°), Kalman-filtered over time, stored fp16 at 0.3°, and applied through the streamed maps with full 2-D bilinear map resampling. Requires `opencv-python-headless` (seam flow is skipped without it). This one field handles every case the earlier staged design needed separate hacks for — the operator's own body near the nadir, close feet, a person straddling the seam — because a true 2-D flow moves each of those coherently instead of shearing or duplicating.

- **Seam-flow measurement reads the `.LRF` sidecar when present** — it is verified to be the two fisheyes side by side at 1024×1024 (corr 1.000 against the downscaled mains), so the whole-clip measurement costs seconds instead of a second 3840² HEVC decode. Falls back to decoding the main streams (hstacked into one rawvideo pipe) when no LRF matches.

- **The `.LRF` sidecar is a dual-fisheye proxy, not a stitched equirectangular one.** It is useful for triage (you can see what is in the shot at 2048x1024 H.264) but it is not a preview of the finished panorama and cannot serve as a stitched reference.

- **The warp is folded into the remap maps themselves and streamed per frame** over **one** fifo (`rawvideo gray16le`, the four x/y maps stacked vertically into a single 4×height frame that ffmpeg `split`s and `crop`s back into the four `remap` inputs) into the same ffmpeg graph — no extra resample stage, same 10-bit chain, same single nearest-neighbour sampling as the static path. ffmpeg's `displace` filter was rejected for this: it silently negotiates everything down to 8-bit `rgb24`. A `SeamFlowWriter` producer thread rewrites only the ±16° column windows around each seam per frame and one writer thread pastes them into a persistent stacked buffer; sentinel-value (65535) map pixels are left static so the warp never samples outside a lens's valid region. **The maps must travel as one stream.** The first design used four fifos (one per map) and deadlocked at the *last frame* — twice at frame 177/179 of concurrent 3840 renders, then at frame 6595/6596 of a 14-hour 8K render: ffmpeg's graph scheduler pulls lens-0 map frames a few frames ahead of lens-1's, and once that drift exceeds the slack in the chain (2-packet demux queue + writer queue) ffmpeg blocks reading the next lens-0 frame while the lens-1 writers block on fifos it is not draining and the producer blocks on the full lens-1 queue, so the EOF sentinel can never be sent. (The 8K render was rescued by hand-feeding one throwaway frame into the starved fifo from a shell — `head -c 58982400 /dev/zero > …/fy0` — which let ffmpeg reach the video EOF and finish normally.) With one stacked frame per video frame the four maps are inseparable and the cycle is impossible. Keep the writer queue shallow (3): every map frame pushed ahead of the video is a 236 MB frame buffered inside ffmpeg.

- **The map streaming sends only seam-window columns** — the producer queues ~10 MB of window slices per frame and each fifo writer pastes them into its own persistent full-frame buffer. The earlier design queued four full 59 MB map copies per frame, which at 8K pushed the whole pipeline into the OOM killer on a 23 GB box.

## Stabilisation

- **IMU stabilisation is on by default: level horizon + smoothed heading** (`--stabilize smooth`; `horizon` = level only with the raw heading, `lock` = world-locked heading (DJI *Free View*, tripod-like shots), `none` = the camera's own tilt). The output is meant to need **no NLE stabiliser** — FCP's 2-D stabiliser shifts the flat equirect frame, cannot wrap the sphere and leaves a 1-px black line at the ±180° edge; a 360 rotation has no crop, so all of this is free. Validated on the walking clip (198–204 s, far-scenery band, frame-to-frame phase correlation): raw camera 0.42°/0.13° per frame (horizontal/vertical), **`smooth` 0.031°/0.014°, DJI Studio's own Direction Lock export 0.025°/0.013°** — the same.

- **The orientation stream is ~1 kHz, not one sample per frame.** `djmd` path `top.3 → 3.3.2.{1,2}`: field 1 = timestamp (µs) of the record's *first* sample, field 2 = frame counter, field 3 = **~33 items per frame**, each four tagged float32 = a unit quaternion `[w,x,y,z]`, ~0.04° apart. `read_orientation()` returns every item, times spread to the next record. The original reading took only the first item — the orientation at the *start* of each frame.

- **The IMU samples are timestamped ~9 ms before the frame they belong to** (`STAB_IMU_OFFSET = 0.009`, added to each frame's IMU sampling time). Measured against a visual gyro (below) on a `--start 198` snippet: leftover tilt after levelling 0.106°/frame at the scan's origin, **0.022° at the minimum**, sharp and symmetric. The scan read "+40 ms" in the snippet's own frame times — and that number was applied to the first full-length render, which came out with 0.09°/frame of residual tilt while the same seconds rendered as a snippet were clean. Cause: **ffmpeg's accurate seek emits the first frame at or after `--start`** (198.0 → source frame 5935 at 198.0312 s; 60.0 → frame 1799 at 60.0266 s), so a seeked snippet's frame *i* is up to one frame later than `start + i/fps`. The snippet's IMU sampling was therefore 31 ms early relative to the true frames, the scan's +40 ms compensated for that, and the true offset is 40 − 31 = 9 ms. `stitch_one` now samples the IMU at the seeked run's real first-frame time (`ceil(start·fps)/fps`), so snippets and full renders are identical. Lesson: whenever a `--start` snippet is used to calibrate anything time-related, pin down which source frame it actually begins with (exact-pixel match against an un-seeked decode with `-fps_mode passthrough` on both sides — cfr fills fake it otherwise).

- **The IMU→equirect transport was MEASURED with a visual gyro, and the hand-derived one was wrong.** Method: ORB features on an *unstabilised* render, unprojected to the sphere, RANSAC-Kabsch per frame pair → the camera's true per-frame rotation; the fixed rotation mapping the IMU's rotations onto it fitted (AX = XB) and refined by minimising the tilt left over after levelling. It converged to an **exact axis permutation** (`IMU_TO_EQUIRECT`: body X→−Z, Y→−X, Z→+Y, every entry within 0.2°), with the quaternion taken as body→world (matrix transposed to bring world vectors into the body frame) and gravity = body −Z. The previous transport (`WORLD_ROT` reflection + x flip) had body X and Y swapped: it corrected a camera roll as if it were a pitch and vice versa, so on a standing, swaying wearer the *levelled* output shook more than the raw one (visual tilt jitter 0.454 vs 0.313°/frame). Its horizon-tilt *magnitude* matched DJI, which was all the earlier validation checked — the tilt's azimuth never was. Lesson: validate a rotation convention with a full 3-DOF visual measurement, not a scalar.

- **The levelling rotation is built directly as pitch-then-roll, with no yaw** (`horizon_rot()`). The shortest-arc rotation would also spin about the vertical, and stripping yaw from it afterwards leaves a large residual tilt because the three Euler angles are coupled — verified: residual tilt < 0.01° on every test frame, yaw exactly 0.

- **Heading smoothing** (`smooth` mode): the camera heading ψ is measured per frame with `heading_deg()` from the *very levelling matrix applied to the picture* (a fixed world horizontal axis transported and levelled, so whatever imperfections `L` has are included), unwrapped over the whole IMU track (so a `--start/--duration` snippet is identical to the same seconds of a full render), low-passed with a zero-phase gaussian (`smooth_heading`, sigma `--stab-smooth`), and the per-frame correction is `Ry(Δ) @ L` with Δ = yaw_sign·(ψ_smooth − ψ) — the yaw applied in the **levelled** frame, about the true vertical (`_euler_to_rot(Δ, pitch, roll)` would be `L @ Ry(Δ)`, a spin about the camera's tilted axis: second order for small Δ, wrong by twice the tilt in `lock` mode). `STAB_YAW_SIGN = +1` was established by rendering both signs: the right one cut the far-band jitter, the wrong one doubled it (with the old axis-swapped transport the sign came out −1 — it flips with the frame). **Sigma 1.0 s reproduces DJI Studio's Direction Lock**: two independent fits — DJI's measured heading jitter on 150–190 s (0.494°/frame vs 0.967 raw) matches a gaussian of σ≈0.9–1.0 s, and a direct fit of DJI's heading deviation from raw peaks at σ=1.0 s with gain −1.07. On a walk with turns the correction reaches tens of degrees (rms 15°, max 68° on this clip) — that is the smoothing rounding real turns, not an error; in 360 it costs nothing.

- **DJI Studio's import dialog offers "Free View" vs "Direction Lock".** *Free View* levels the horizon **and** world-locks heading (our `lock`). *Direction Lock* levels the horizon and keeps the device's front perspective, smoothed (our `smooth`). **Always export the reference with Direction Lock when validating**; against Free View the yaw offset swings ±175° across a walking clip because heading is being compared against a world-locked frame.

- **The old `--stab-smooth` (box-averaging the levelling matrices) is gone** — it let the horizon float. The horizon is always levelled exactly; only the heading is smoothed.

- **Earlier validation numbers** (horizon tracks DJI 9.4 vs 9.2°… on the most-tilted frame) measured only the tilt *magnitude* via the horizon sinusoid fit and were consistent with the axis-swapped transport; they are superseded by the visual-gyro jitter numbers above. The whole-frame correlation metric remains unreliable on near-symmetric frames.

## How the rotation is applied

- **It is applied as a second resample after the stitch**, because the remap maps are static — either by ffmpeg's `v360=e:e` driven per frame by `sendcmd` (`--rotator v360`, the default without a GPU) or by the script's own `EquirectRotator` on the GPU (`--rotator torch`, the default wherever torch sees an Apple/CUDA GPU; see the next bullets). Two facts about `v360` were **measured with synthetic markers rather than assumed**: (1) `sendcmd` *composes* each command with the current state rather than replacing it (sending `pitch 20` twice gives 40); (2) v360's own convention is `R = Rz(roll)·Rx(pitch)·Ry(−yaw)` — same order as ours, yaw sign flipped, so yaw is negated on the way out.

- **Do not send per-frame deltas to compensate for (1). Use `reset_rot`.** The first version sent deltas from the previous target. That drifted: the Euler decomposition of a small delta is not exactly that delta rotation, the chain accumulated error frame to frame, and it landed precisely where the two lenses cross-fade — **straight fence posts bent into arcs at both seams**, a half-circle distortion rising from the lower corners. v360's `reset_rot` is runtime-settable (`T` flag), so every command is now `reset_rot 1` + the **absolute** yaw/pitch/roll. Nothing accumulates. Verified with markers (reset+pitch 20 then reset+pitch 5 → −20 then −5, not −25) and on the real seam (posts straight again, matching DJI). Cost: ~2.5× encode time at 8K with `lanczos`; default is `cubic` (`--stab-interp`), visually equivalent at 8K and ~3× cheaper. (`--stab-interp` only concerns the `v360` rotator; the torch rotator is always v360-cubic.)

- **`v360` regenerates its entire lookup table on every `sendcmd` command, and the stabiliser sends four per frame** (`reset_rot`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `roll`). Measured with a synthetic 8K source on 16 cores: 0.09 s/frame with no commands, **0.22 s per command, 0.99 s/frame with four** — the whole 0.9 fps an M4 Max render sat at, hardware encoder idle; `linear` instead of `cubic` barely helps (0.78) because the cost is per-pixel trig in the table build, not table size. There is no way to send a 3-DOF rotation in fewer commands, so the fix is `EquirectRotator`: the same resample done as one small matmul plus 48 gathers per frame, on the GPU via torch (mps/cuda), or torch-CPU/numpy for validation. Pipeline in that mode: stitch ffmpeg → rawvideo `gbrp10le` pipe → rotate → encode ffmpeg (audio stream-copied by the encoder side straight from the source). `stab_targets()` yields the per-frame matrix `T`; `stab_matrix()` converts it to what v360 would render.

- **Every `EquirectRotator` convention was measured against v360's own output on a real frame, not read off its source.** Output pixel `(i, j)` looks along `phi = ((2i+1)/W − 1)π`, `theta = ((2j+1)/H − 1)π/2` (y down); the rotated direction is read at `u = (phi'/π + 1)(W−1)/2`, `v = (theta'/(π/2) + 1)(H−1)/2` — **v360 scales its input side by `W−1`/`H−1`, so its zero rotation is a 0.5 px shift at the frame edges and 0 at the centre** (found because "identity" v360 output differed from the input by up to 258/1023, with a displacement that regressed to ±0.5 px at the edges); replicated on purpose so the two paths are interchangeable. Kernel: 4×4 **Lagrange** cubic (Keys was 5× worse), sum **truncated** not rounded (rint left 44% of samples 1 LSB high, floor 7%); columns wrap, rows reflect across the poles with a 180° shift. Rotation: v360 applies `M = Ry(yaw)·Rx(pitch)·Rz(roll)` (standard matrices, that product order) to the output direction — found by brute force over all orders/signs/transposes — which for the script's target `T` is exactly `M = P·Tᵀ·P`, `P = diag(−1, 1, −1)`; no Euler round trip. Agreement with v360: within 1 LSB on >99.99% of samples (max 5/1023), torch and numpy backends within 1 LSB of each other; end-to-end CRF-16 renders of the validation snippet differ by encoder noise only (46.8 dB, zero systematic shift to 0.001 px, no frame offset).

## GPU path and throughput

- **The stitch itself (`remap`×2 + `maskedmerge`) also runs as a Metal kernel** (`--stitch gpu`, `GpuStitcher`, `stitch_equirect` in `METAL_SRC`), bit-exact with ffmpeg's: nearest-neighbour lens lookup, the 65535 sentinel (−1 as int16) fills black, and the blend is `(a·(1023−m) + b·m + 511) // 1023` with the 8-bit mask scaled ×4 exactly as ffmpeg's gray→gbrp10 conversion does (255 → 1020, so ffmpeg's "full" blend is 99.7% — reproduced, not fixed). `stitch_frame_np` is the numpy statement of it (100% exact against ffmpeg on a real frame pair; the Metal kernel 100% exact against numpy on the Mac, `tools/rotator_bench.py --osv`). The static maps live on the GPU; per frame only the ~33 MB of seam windows from `SeamFlowWriter` (now a 4-worker pool, `fifo=False`) are uploaded in one copy. ffmpeg then only decodes (both lenses `format=gbrp10le`, `vstack`ed into one rawvideo frame) and, in a second process, encodes. Measured on a CPU-only Linux box why this was needed: with the ffmpeg graph, decode was 86 ms/frame, the fifo-streamed 236 MB map frame ~50 ms, the remaps/blend ~25 ms and the 177 MB pipe more — the Mac sat at 3.9 fps with the GPU rotation alone, 10.3 with the GPU stitch.

- **Transport between the three processes matters as much as the kernels** (`--transport`, default `mixed`): measured with `tools/transport_bench.py` on macOS, a pipe carries an 8K frame in 23 ms, a unix-domain socket in 109 ms with default buffers but 8 ms once the *sender* sets a multi-MB `SO_SNDBUF`. Only this process can set that (not ffmpeg), so frames go decoder→here over a pipe and here→encoder over a socket; an all-socket run fell to 5 fps waiting on the decoder. VideoToolbox itself encodes 8K at 33 fps and the RGB→4:2:0 conversion in front of it costs nothing, so what remains on the encoder side is its socket read — which `--jobs 2` can double up by running two whole pipelines on two halves of the clip (opt-in: at 8K the second pipeline's in-flight 177 MB frames plus the GPU allocator pushed a 48 GB Mac into swap) (each seeks to half a frame before its first frame, stops after `-frames:v`, `SeamFlowWriter(frame_offset=…)`, the stabiliser targets sliced from the single whole-run list; segments are concat-demuxed with stream copy and the audio muxed once — verified frame-exact against a single run, no dip at the join).

## Pipeline rules that were learned the hard way

- **The map and mask inputs must be single-frame inputs, never `loop`ed, and every input sits on one frame-index timeline (`setpts=N/FRAME_RATE/TB`).** For a long time the static maps and the mask went through `loop=-1:1:0`; each looped frame was an extra framesync event (~1.8 output frames per source frame — the `drop=149` on a 179-frame clip in ffmpeg's stats was the tell), and the output vsync's dedup then kept the duplicate and dropped the *real* frame about once in six. Every early render, including the first full 8K one, had this **5 Hz judder** (a frame shown twice, the next skipped): it read as camera shake, was invisible to frame counts, and polluted every jitter measurement. Framesync repeats a secondary input's last frame after EOF on its own, so a one-frame map input is all `remap` needs. Sanity check on any pipeline change: the ffmpeg stats line must end with `drop=0` (or no drop at all) and a frame-to-frame difference scan of the output must show no near-zero pairs on moving footage.

- **DJI Studio re-encodes audio** (317 kbps → 110 kbps on the tested clip) and relabels 29.97 fps as 30. `osv2mp4` stream-copies audio and preserves the source frame rate.
